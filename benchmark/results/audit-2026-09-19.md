# Jev benchmark audit, 2026-09-19

Scope: independent verification of the Jev-vs-chat-model comparison, prompted by an
external first-pass review. Seven parallel investigations against the real code and the
171-window probability cache. No API calls except the live ablation noted below.

Every number here was reproduced from `src/benchmark/` and `results/raw/`, not taken from
the review or from `results/report-combined.md`.

## 1. Verdict

The headline "Jev beats chat LLMs at ad detection" is not supported.

| comparison | jev F0.5 | haiku F0.5 | diff | t(11) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as published, jev tuned in-sample, 1 pass | 0.9572 | 0.9077 | +0.0495 | +1.887 |
| jev cross-validated over its real selection surface | 0.9139 | 0.9077 | +0.0063 | +0.131 |
| jev measured at 5 passes, shipped thresholds | 0.9190 | 0.9077 | +0.0113 | see 2a |

One-sided critical value at 11 df is 1.796. The published version clears it by 0.09.
Two-sided it never clears. After cross-validating the parameters chosen by looking at
this corpus, the difference is +0.006 F0.5 on 12 episodes with a paired standard error
of 0.048.

`README.md:245` already states this in prose: "Do not read that as Jev beating Haiku."
The generated table in `results/report-combined.md` contradicts its own README.

The honest claim, which the evidence does support:

> On a 14-episode corpus, Jev's per-segment decomposition is statistically
> indistinguishable from claude-haiku-4-5 at roughly 1/37 the cost and 1/59 the latency,
> and never cuts audio that contains no advertising. It removes about 3x more editorial
> content than Haiku does, because segment quantization makes every boundary error cost a
> whole 26-second segment.

## 2a. Self-consistency makes jev worse, not better

Measured 2026-09-19, `benchmark jev-spike --oracle off --passes 5`, 855 fresh windows.

| | F1 | F0.5 | P | R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 pass (published) | 0.9294 | 0.9572 | 0.9792 | 0.8931 |
| 5 passes, same thresholds | 0.8960 | 0.9190 | 0.9380 | 0.8650 |

`aggregate_passes` means the per-segment probabilities before assembly. The single-pass
distribution is sharply bimodal (3839 of 4228 answers below 0.40, 210 at or above 0.95), so
averaging moves mass toward the middle and fewer segments clear the 0.95 `enter` gate.
Precision falls 0.979 to 0.938, recall 0.893 to 0.865.

`ENTER_THRESHOLD` was fitted to the single-pass distribution and does not transfer to
averaged probabilities. This is a ninth entry for the selection surface in section 7: the
value of `--passes` interacts with the thresholds, and `README.md:380-395` records the
sweep as "all identical", which no longer holds.

Deliberately NOT re-tuned. Sweeping `enter` against the averaged probabilities on the same
12 episodes would reopen exactly the optimism problem this audit documents. The constants
stay frozen and the result is reported as measured.

Note that 0.9190 lands next to the cross-validated 0.9139, which is mild corroboration that
0.9572 was the optimistic number rather than the representative one.

## 3. At IoU 0.5 the margin is inside the noise

Bootstrap, 10,000 draws, resampling the 12 ad-bearing episodes with replacement, paired.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| jev vs haiku[segment_ids] trial 1 | 0.5 | +0.0056 | 0.601 | [-0.0283, +0.0438] |
| jev vs haiku[timestamps], as published | 0.5 | +0.0495 | 0.979 | [+0.0021, +0.1000] |

The published interval excludes zero by two thousandths of a point. Per-episode record is

compared against a distribution wider than the effect being claimed.

## 4. What jev actually is

Per-trial means summed over the 12 ad episodes, 49 canonical truth spans.

| model | pred spans | missed | spurious | sloppy (hit@0.5, miss@0.8) | sloppy % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jev-ceiling (oracle) | 48.0 | 1.0 | 0.0 | 3.0 | 6% |
| jev | 45.0 | 5.0 | 1.0 | 11.0 | 25% |
| claude-haiku-4-5 | 52.6 | 3.2 | 6.8 | 7.6 | 17% |
| qwen3.5-plus-02-15 | 52.0 | 4.4 | 7.4 | 5.4 | 12% |
| google/gemini-3.5-flash | 54.2 | 4.0 | 9.2 | 6.8 | 15% |

Jev emits 1 spurious span where the chat models emit 6 to 10. That single number buys the
0.9572, because F0.5 weights precision 2x and IoU 0.5 prices boundary error at zero.

But jev also misses more ads than Haiku (5 of 49 vs 3.2) and cuts the ones it finds more
loosely than anything else in the top 13 except grok-4.3.

So the profile is not "better". It is different: jev almost never hallucinates an ad, at
the cost of missing more of them and cutting all of them sloppily. Whether that wins is a
product decision about the price of a false positive against a loose boundary. The
benchmark's metric currently makes that decision silently.

## 5. Error decomposition

A three-level ladder separating granularity from assembly from judgment. The "ceiling" row
in the existing report cannot do this, because it runs the same assembly code as the live
row and only swaps the probabilities.

L0 truth -> L1 truth snapped to segment edges -> L2 oracle labels through the real
assembler -> L3 live jev.

At IoU 0.8, jev's 0.2781 F1 shortfall from perfect splits:

| component | F1 delta | share |
|---|---|---|
| granularity (L0 to L1) | 0.0583 | 21.0% |
| assembly (L1 to L2) | 0.0296 | 10.7% |
| judgment (L2 to L3) | 0.1901 | 68.4% |

Judgment costs 6.4x what assembly does. The entire assembly delta is one episode: span
lists are byte-identical on 11 of 12, and only `ep-tosh-show` diverges.

That assembly residual is irreducible, not a tuning failure. `max_gap` swept from 0 to 90s
in 0.1s steps never reproduces L1, because the corpus contains a direct contradiction:

- `ep-security-now` has a 28.93s pure-silence wall INSIDE one truth ad (needs gap >= 28.93)
- `ep-tosh-show` has a 27.72s pure-silence wall BETWEEN two distinct ads (needs gap < 27.72)

`_bridged` and the 15s canonicalization gap contribute exactly zero at every value tested.

Caveat on the granularity row: the corpus truth is authored on the segment grid. 53 of 53
raw truth ad starts land exactly on a segment start. The 21% granularity share is a floor;
against independently annotated truth it would be materially larger.

### Judgment failure anatomy

- Spurious spans: 0. Every one of jev's 45 spans lands on a real ad. All 12 "false
  positives" at IoU 0.8 are overlapping spans that fail the threshold, not hallucinations.
- Missed entirely: 4 of 49. Three are episode-opening pre-rolls.
- Boundary errors are bimodal, not Gaussian: 36 of 45 starts and 36 of 45 ends are exact
  within 1s; the other 9 are off by 5 to 72s. The failure mode is "one shoulder segment got
  the wrong label", not gradual drift.
- Concentration: `drink-champs` alone carries 27.4% of judgment loss, the top 3 episodes
  carry 71.2%, and 4 of 12 episodes contribute zero or negative.

## 6. Confirmed bug: span truncation at jev.py:351

`spans_from_probabilities` sets a span's end to `members[-1]["end"]`, the last member's
end, rather than the maximum end over members. Because `jev.py:323` sorts by start, a
segment that strictly contains the following segment truncates the span.

Live case, `ep-security-now-audio-2850b24903b2`, segments 108/109:

| | span |
|---|---|
| as shipped | 2560.07 - 2664.88 |
| with max-end | 2560.07 - 2687.39 |

Truth is 2560.07 - 2693.01. 22.51s of a real ad break left in; that episode's IoU goes
0.7884 -> 0.9577.

Corpus-wide impact of the fix: neutral at IoU 0.5/0.6/0.7, +0.0119 F1 and F0.5 at IoU
0.8/0.9, end MAE -0.268s at IoU 0.5. Small because the pattern only bites when the
contained segment is a run's last member, which happens once in 45 spans.

It should land on correctness grounds regardless. There are 15 strict superset/subset pairs
corpus-wide and the largest truncation available is 27.11s (`tim-dillon` sids 114/113).

The analogous stale-frontier forms at `jev.py:339` and `jev.py:379` change nothing on this
corpus but have the same shape and were fixed for symmetry.

### Status: fixed 2026-09-19

All three forms corrected in `spans_from_probabilities` and `_bridged`: the span end is now
the furthest end over run members, the `max_gap` comparison tracks a running frontier, and
the bridge wall measures from the previous run's furthest end. Four tests added as
`tests/test_jev.py::TestContainedSegments`; reverting any one of the three fixes fails
exactly one of them.

Suite 285 -> 289 passed. Measured effect, single pass:

| IoU | F1 before | F1 after | F0.5 before | F0.5 after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.9294 | 0.9294 | 0.9572 | 0.9572 |
| 0.7 | 0.8484 | 0.8484 | 0.8749 | 0.8749 |
| 0.8 | 0.7219 | 0.7338 | 0.7484 | 0.7603 |
| 0.9 | 0.6892 | 0.7011 | 0.7156 | 0.7275 |

Both controls still PASS. Worth noting: post-fix jev at IoU 0.8 scores F0.5 0.7603, which
is claude-haiku-4-5's IoU-0.8 figure to four decimals. A dead heat at strict matching.

Root cause of the duplicates is upstream, not the benchmark:
`src/transcriber.py:353 merge_overlapping_segments` keeps a new-chunk segment
unconditionally when it starts inside the 30s overlap but extends past it. The overlaps are
periodic at 1318s spacing, exactly chunk duration minus `CHUNK_OVERLAP_SECONDS`.

### The asymmetry

Jev had 8 configuration choices fitted against these 12 episodes, 2 of them
cross-validated. The chat models had zero: `git log -- prompts/` shows one commit
(02b90d8f, 2026-08-13), never edited. No content retries, no model-specific prompt
variants, no model branching anywhere in `src/ad_detector/`.

## 8. Confounds that do NOT exist

Three things that look like they should be unfair to jev, and are not.

**Granularity handicap.** 93.3% of chat-model start boundaries and 89.3% of end boundaries
already sit within 0.05s of a segment edge, because the prompt renders segments as
`[{start:.1f}s - {end:.1f}s]` and the models copy the numbers back. Snapping every chat
prediction to jev's grid moves mean F0.5 by +0.0004 at IoU 0.5 and -0.0003 at IoU 0.8.
Both sides already work on the same grid, and jev's boundaries are the worse ones.

**Parse failures.** claude-haiku-4-5 had 0 failed windows out of 855. Excluding
parse-failure windows changes the top 5 chat models' F0.5 by +0.000 each. The confound is
devastating at the bottom of the table (`bytedance-seed/seed-2-1-turbo` loses 99.0% of ad
opportunities) but absent at the top.

**Trial-count fairness.** `_recompute_costs` runs per (model, episode, trial) and averages
over trials before summing, so the published chat cost is already one-pass-equivalent.

Two confounds run the other way, in the chat models' favor:

- The chat prompt ships a 296-brand sponsor gazetteer. 42 of those brands appear verbatim
  inside this corpus's truth spans, including 12 in `ep-security-now`.
- The chat side is asked a strictly harder question (find the ad, name its category,
  transcribe boundary timestamps, emit schema-valid JSON) and scored on part of it.

## 9. Reporting defects

Three defects previously believed fixed have not landed.

**Cost normalization is still split.** `jev.py:820` divides by episode count;
`aggregate.py:420-422` accumulates a corpus total. Same column, two denominators, 14x
apart. A report regenerated today prints jev at $0.0021 against haiku at $1.0820, a 515x
ratio. The true like-for-like figure is 36.6x ($0.029565 vs $1.0820 corpus-wide, or
$0.002112 vs $0.07729 per episode).

`README.md:250-251` claims "roughly 1% of the cost". Actual is 2.73%.

The column label at `report-combined.md:31` ("Average dollars per episode") is wrong for
every row, not just jev. Divide every published figure by 14.

`results/report-combined.md` is internally inconsistent mid-migration: `$0.0296` at lines
59, 152, 1448, 1556 and `$0.0021` at line 982, same row, same file.

**Latency still publishes 0.0s.** All 171 cache entries have `elapsed_ms == 0`. The guard
at `jev.py:840` is correct and writes nothing, but `ModelStats.p50_call_latency_ms`
defaults to 0.0 (`aggregate.py:103-107`) and `sections.py:104` / `sections.py:815-816`
render it unconditionally. There is no "unavailable" path. Net effect is identical to the
hardcoded zero that was thought to be removed: jev is published as the fastest system in
the benchmark, ahead of `google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite` at 0.7s, and the latency-tail table
sorts by p50 so jev sits at the top of it.
Affected: `report-combined.md:59`, `:713`, `:714`, `:1448`, `:1556-1557`.

**Determinism published 0.0000 from n=1.** Corrected on inspection: the current code
already skips a row with no `f1_stdev_per_episode`, so the `jev` / `jev-ceiling` 0.0000
rows at `report-combined.md:991-992` are stale output from an earlier version. The defect
is real but differently shaped: `metrics.trial_stdev` returns 0.0 for n < 2
(`metrics.py:303-305`), so any single-trial row publishes a fabricated 0.0000 as if
measured. That table also never sorted by stdev; it sorts by mean F1
(`sections.py:881`), so jev led it on accuracy, not on determinism.

**Pricing provenance.** `INPUT_COST_PER_MTOK = 0.042` (`jev.py:30-31`) is sourced to a URL
in a comment, with no committed snapshot, unlike every chat model. Output cost is
unmodeled; jev produced 79,098 output tokens (11.2% of its input). Even at 10x input
pricing jev stays 17x cheaper, so the cost advantage is real. Only its magnitude is wrong.

**Provenance.** `results/report-combined.md` is untracked and `src/benchmark/jev.py` has no
git history at all. No constant's origin can be traced through commits.

**Doc error.** `report.md:45` and `report-combined.md:45` say episodes are split into
"~85-second" windows. Measured lengths are 410-595s, mean ~550s. Off by about 6.5x in the
section explaining the methodology.

### Why the proposed patch should not land as written

Beyond the value of `lead`:

1. `lead > enter` is unguarded. When no member reaches `lead`, the span collapses to the
   run's final and weakest segment. 10 of the 35 points in `THRESHOLD_GRID`
   (`jev.py:661-665`) have `enter < lead`, so `tune_thresholds` and `cross_validate` would
   silently evaluate a third of the grid under a broken span builder. `lead=1.0` collapses
   every span in the corpus with no error.
2. `TAIL_THRESHOLD = 0.40` cannot trim anything (see above).
3. Trimming defeats `canonicalize_ads`. Shortening spans grows the gaps between them, so
   pairs that used to merge under the 15s rule no longer do, turning 1 TP into 1 TP + 1 FP.
4. Cross-window dedup undoes the trim. `canonicalize_ads` takes the union and the earliest
   start wins, so whenever one window trims less than the other, the least-trimmed boundary
   is what gets scored.
5. `lead` is reachable only as a module constant. It is not plumbed through
   `score_episode`, `merge_into_stats`, `tune_thresholds`, `cross_validate`, or the CLI.
   The CV numbers in the patch comment cannot have been produced by the patched code.
6. The test suite is 285 pass before and after. `tests/test_jev.py:20-26` pins
   `ENTER, STAY = 0.60, 0.40`, so every span test runs in the broken `lead > enter` regime
   and none catches it.

## 10a. Decomposition ablation: the leading edge is the model

Run 2026-09-19. Haiku scored per segment over the same 171 windows from
`jev.episode_windows`, with `GUIDANCE_FULL` and `NOUL_INSTRUCTIONS` verbatim, assembled by
the unmodified `jev.spans_from_probabilities` at enter 0.95 / stay 0.40. One deterministic
pass, 4228 segment answers, zero missing sids.

| row | model | decomposition | F0.5 @0.5 | F0.5 @0.8 | start bias @0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jev | jev | per-segment + assembler | 0.9572 | 0.7603 | -3.96s |
| haiku-per-segment | haiku | per-segment + assembler | 0.9160 | 0.8261 | -0.07s |
| haiku-segment_ids | haiku | span emission | 0.9050 | 0.7221 | +0.87s |
| jev-ceiling | oracle | per-segment + assembler | 0.9960 | 0.9147 | -1.19s |

Swapping the model under a fixed decomposition moves the start edge 3.89s. Swapping the
decomposition under a fixed model moves it 0.94s. The decomposition's own floor is one
episode: `tosh-show`, where the truth start falls mid-segment and no per-segment method can
cut inside it. Excluding it, the oracle's start bias is exactly 0.00s, jev's is -3.02s and
haiku-per-segment's is +1.22s.

Mechanism. Of the 179 window-instances jev scores in the 0.40-0.95 band, Haiku pushes 70
above 0.95 and 75 below 0.40, keeping 34. On the segment immediately preceding an ad start,
jev has 7 in 0.40-0.95 (4 in 0.40-0.60) against Haiku's 2. A segment at 0.45 cannot open a
run, but `STAY` 0.40 lets an open run extend backwards across it. The two models agree on
the ad body to three decimals (0.870 vs 0.869 at the boundary segment) and diverge only on
the shoulder: jev hedges there, Haiku either commits or collapses to 0.01.

Consequence for section 10. The external review's `lead` threshold addresses a real
symptom in the wrong layer. The mushy shoulder band is a property of jev's scoring, not of
asking one question per segment and gluing runs together, so a second assembly threshold
compensates for the judge instead of fixing it.

Consequence for section 1. At IoU 0.8 haiku-per-segment is the strongest non-oracle row
measured, above jev (0.7603) and above Haiku's own span emission (0.7221). The
decomposition is not what limits this system; the judge is. Running a chat model through
jev's decomposition beats both jev and that same chat model emitting spans directly.

Cost: $0.7536 notional for the corpus against jev's $0.0296, about 18x, since jev bills
input only at $0.042/Mtok and does not bill output. The accuracy gain is real and it is not
free.

Operational note: every failed call returned HTTP 504 from openresty at exactly 90.0s
elapsed, against a 180s client timeout in `benchmark.toml`. The self-hosted wrapper has a
hard gateway timeout below the client's. p50 was 39.2s with a p90 of 67.9s, so the tail
runs into that wall. Raise the proxy timeout or drop concurrency before rerunning.

## 10b. Seconds of audio damage

Threshold-free metric measuring the product's actual harm: seconds of editorial removed and
seconds of ad retained. No IoU anywhere. 12 ad-bearing episodes, 17.484 h of audio, chat
rows averaged over 5 trials so every figure is one-pass-equivalent.

| row | editorial removed /h | spurious /h | overhang /h | ad retained /h | events >30s /h | pct of damage in >30s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jev 1 pass | 14.6s | 0.0s | 14.6s | 21.3s | 0.23 | 62.9% |
| jev 5 passes | 16.8s | 0.0s | 16.8s | 21.3s | 0.29 | 67.7% |
| jev-ceiling (oracle) | 3.6s | 0.0s | 3.6s | 0.0s | 0.00 | 0% |
| haiku-per-segment | 7.2s | 2.8s | 4.5s | 22.9s | 0.00 | 0% |
| claude-haiku-4-5 [timestamps] | 4.7s | 0.9s | 3.8s | 22.6s | 0.00 | 0% |
| qwen3.5-plus-02-15 | 19.2s | 5.0s | 14.2s | 17.5s | 0.15 | 39.5% |
| google/gemini-3.5-flash | 14.0s | 4.1s | 9.9s | 19.5s | 0.05 | 13.9% |

All 12 rows predicted zero spans on both no-ad controls in every trial, so controls do not
separate anything here.

Two findings, pointing opposite ways.

jev never cuts blind. Its spurious damage is exactly 0.0 s/h: across 17.5 hours it never
removed a stretch of audio containing no advertising. Every wrongly removed second was
attached to a real ad break. Every chat row sits at 0.9 to 5.0 s/h of fully hallucinated
cuts, including the haiku port of jev's own prompt shape at 2.8 s/h. The segment-edge
construction is what buys this.

jev's errors are nonetheless the worse shape. It removes 14.6 s/h of editorial against
claude-haiku-4-5's 4.7 s/h, roughly 3x, and 62.9% of that damage sits in events longer than
30 seconds. jev's median editorial event is 27.3s; the chat models' median audible event is
0 to 2s. Both haiku rows produce zero >30s events. Paired bootstrap over 10,000 resamples:
P(jev does less damage) = 0.005 against haiku[timestamps] and 0.024 against
haiku-per-segment, both intervals excluding zero. jev is Pareto-dominated on the
editorial/ad trade under both pooled and macro normalization.

The mechanism is quantization. Because boundaries snap to ~26s segments, every boundary
error costs a whole segment. A chat model can be wrong by 2 seconds; jev cannot. So the
intuition that a hallucinated span is catastrophic and a boundary error is cheap inverts
here: jev is the few-large-errors system and the chat models are the many-small-errors
systems. For audio that gets physically cut, many-small is preferable, since a 27s error
deletes a paragraph of the show and a 2s error clips a word.

About a quarter of jev's editorial damage is a floor it cannot cross: jev-ceiling, a perfect
per-segment judge through the same assembler, still leaks 3.6 s/h of overhang and 62.8s
corpus-wide, purely from segment granularity.

The trade stated in units, per episode: jev costs $0.075 less and finishes about 285s
sooner, removes 14.5 more seconds of the listener's show (21.3s against 6.8s), and leaves
about the same amount of ad in (21.3 against 22.6 s/h). That is a cost-and-latency-for-
quality trade, not a free win.

Also settles the multi-pass question. jev 1 pass and 5 passes differ in exactly one span
corpus-wide: on ep-the-brilliant-idiots the 5-pass version extends 38.3s earlier into
editorial. That one span is the entire F0.5 drop from 0.9572 to 0.9193, and ad retained is
identical between them. Single pass is strictly better.

Cache preserved at `results/raw/haiku_persegment_cache.json` (171 windows, 4228 segment
answers) so the ablation and this metric are reproducible without re-spending.

## 10c. Tail risk, not means

The 10b figures are means over 5 trials. Means are the wrong summary for a product whose
risk is borne one listener at a time. Recomputed treating each (episode, trial) as one draw,
60 draws per chat row and 60 for jev scored as five independent passes.

| row | mean s/h | p90 s/h | worst draw s/h | largest cut s | draws with a >30s cut | with a >60s cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jev (5 passes as draws) | 24.9 | 65.0 | 104.5 | 46.5 | 18/60 | 0/60 |
| claude-haiku-4-5 [timestamps] | 5.8 | 18.0 | 41.1 | 26.5 | 0/60 | 0/60 |
| haiku-per-segment | 7.9 | 25.6 | 40.2 | 27.7 | 0/12 | 0/12 |
| qwen3.5-plus | 24.3 | 63.4 | 72.9 | 70.1 | 9/60 | 5/60 |
| grok-4.3 | 18.5 | 60.7 | 146.7 | 69.9 | 17/60 | 5/60 |
| gemini-3.5-flash-lite | 8.6 | 18.0 | 100.2 | 133.7 | 2/60 | 1/60 |
| sonnet-4-6 | 18.7 | 58.8 | 60.7 | 64.4 | 9/60 | 2/60 |

Chat models do cut show content, and the worst single event in the study is theirs:
gemini-3.5-flash-lite removed 133.7s contiguously on ep-the-tim-dillon-show trial 3
(2286.3-2420.0s), 126.0s of it audible speech, 2.9x jev's worst. Across all rows there are
65 cuts over 30s, from 7 of 8 chat rows, and 5 of 8 produced a cut over 60s. jev produced
none over 60s in 60 draws. Restricted to audible speech, jev's worst cut anywhere is 35.0s
and five chat rows exceed it.

Distribution shape, largest cut over median cut: jev 1.7, haiku[timestamps] 883, grok-4.3
2331, gemini-3.5-flash-lite 4457. jev has no tail; it is the 20-45s bucket. The chat rows
concentrate near zero with an open tail that occasionally fires, so a low mean can conceal a
draw that deletes two minutes of the show.

Correction to section 10b's framing: jev's damage is NOT bounded by construction. The
assembler chains segments without a cap, extending across any neighbour above STAY within
MAX_RUN_GAP_SECONDS and bridging a further BRIDGE_SECONDS. jev's 46.5s cut spans two
segments in an episode whose largest segment is 29.5s, so the one-segment bound is already
broken. What jev has is quantisation, not boundedness.

None of this rescues jev against the actual comparator. claude-haiku-4-5 [timestamps]
produced zero >30s cuts in all 60 draws across 175 cuts, largest 26.5s, and wins every risk
framing computed: mean, median, p90, CVaR at the 90th percentile (31.7 vs 91.3 s/h),
P(draw exceeds 30s or 60s of total removal), adversarial-episode damage, and smallest worst
cut. A structural zero carries no sampling variance, which is why the episode bootstrap
backs that contrast at P = 0.0000.

Identifiability, stated plainly: 12 episodes cannot identify a tail. Every p90 confidence
interval is wider than its own point estimate, and the `max` statistic is pinned to the
observation by construction. gemini-3.5-flash-lite's 133.7s cut has a bootstrap CI of
[16.4, 133.7]s, resting on one episode appearing in the resample. Any claim of the form
"model X's worst case is N seconds" from this corpus describes one or two events, not the
model. The only tail claims that should carry weight in a decision are the structural zeros
and the >30s-cut rate contrast, CI [+0.067, +0.550].

## 11. What would settle it

The corpus cannot resolve a difference of this size. 12 ad-bearing episodes, 49 truth
spans, paired SE 0.048.

1. A held-out corpus: 10 or more ad-bearing episodes captured after the constants were
   frozen, scored at the frozen 0.95/0.40/30/30 with no re-tuning. At $0.0021 per episode
   of jev spend this is a cheap experiment and it is the only thing that would settle the
   question.
2. `BRIDGE_SECONDS` and the state-variant dimension inside the CV, with all four variant
   payloads cached.
3. Real `elapsed_ms` in the jev cache, so the latency column is a measurement.
4. Either multiple jev trials or a blanked determinism cell, so 0.0000 is not a default
   rendered as a result.
5. One normalization for the cost column, applied to both sides.
6. A committed pricing source for $0.042/Mtok and a stated basis for "output is not billed".

## 12. Method

Seven independent investigations, each re-deriving its numbers from `src/benchmark/` and
`results/raw/` rather than from the published report or the external review. Harness
self-checks required each to reproduce jev F1 0.9294 / F0.5 0.9572 at IoU 0.5 and the
report's IoU-0.5 rows before any new result was accepted; the ranking rebuild matched
`report-combined.md` on 86 of 86 rows exactly.

Two corrections surfaced between investigations and are reflected above: strict
superset/subset pairs number 15, not 19; and jev's F0.5 at IoU 0.8 is 0.7484, not 0.7482.

Boundary MAE and bias computed at high IoU are survivor-biased, since only pairs matching
at that threshold contribute. Only the IoU 0.5 boundary row is comparable across variants.
